1.2 The Delper Solution
Delper Ecom Global resolves this structural fragmentation by building a parallel capital engine. This system converts verified, daily human attention into non-speculative physical goods and self-sustained smart cities. The platform does not rely on volatile external cash flows or retail speculative markets. Instead, it utilizes an automated, closed-loop economic model to transform programmatic ad revenue into real-world consumer staples, global B2B trade networks, and sovereign-backed infrastructure assets.

3.2 The Immutable 30-30-30-10 Revenue Split
Daily family interactions generate a reliable gross ad-revenue yield of approximately $823 (₹64,800) per household monthly. This yield is split through on-chain escrow accounts using an immutable formula:
  • 30% (Direct Family Rewards): Formally converted into non-transferable internal ecosystem credits. These rewards can only be used for cashless redemptions of groceries, household staples, and electronic devices. External cash withdrawals and token dumps are entirely barred.
  • 30% (Infrastructure Operations): Reserved for ongoing system maintenance, AWS cloud-native data systems, AI-driven content analysis, and Layer-2 blockchain transaction gas.
  • 30% (Corporate Gross Profit): Retained by the company to secure vendor fulfillment contracts, purchase land, and fund physical infrastructure projects.
  • 10% (Corporate Social Responsibility & Buffers): Routed to regional non-profits (such as the Devaramakkalu Charitable Trust) to manage community development and fund emergency food reserves.
4. INTERCOMPANY TREASURY CONTROLS & REINVESTMENT FLOWS
To maintain financial stability and ensure strict alignment with international transfer pricing rules and India's Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), the flow of funds follows structured rules:
4.1 Subsidiary Earnings Model
  • Local Capital Management: Local subsidiaries, led by Delper Ecom Private Limited (India), collect regional advertising revenues, manage local micro-hubs, and pay state corporate taxes. They maintain a steady internal EBITDA of 3%.
  • Upstream Capital Routing: Surplus operational cash flows above the local 3% EBITDA cap are sent up as corporate dividends to the parent entity, Delper Global Holdings Pte. Ltd. (Singapore).
4.2 Holding Company Management and Dividend Lockups
  • Retained Corporate Yield: The Singapore holding company coordinates macro corporate operations and retains a strict 5% EBITDA reserve to meet local tax commitments.
  • Investor Payout Loop: The holding company distributes 95% of the remaining net balance profit as structured investment dividends to anchor investor GK Bharta.
  • The Automated Reinvestment Safeguard: To maximize early-stage enterprise growth, a binding capital guardrail requires that 100% of these distributed investor profits are fully reinvested back into the operating ecosystem until regional hubs and smart cities achieve complete self-sustaining financial break-even.

8. STRATEGIC COMPLIANCE & RISK VECTOR MITIGATION
8.1 Landowner Equity-Swap Protections
To address land-use conflicts in the Bidadi/Byramangala sericultural corridor, the development applies a 60:40 land-pooling system. Displaced landowners receive non-dilutable equity blocks in the Delper Pre-IPO distribution tranche, converting local stakeholders into long-term co-owners of the development.
8.2 Double Taxation Avoidance Optimization
  • India-Singapore DTAA Compatibility: Capital distributions, technology licensing fees, and dividend flows traveling from Delper Ecom Pvt. Ltd. up to Singapore are structured under the India-Singapore Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) to minimize withholding tax exposures.
  • POEM Guardrails: By establishing key board decisions, executive management controls, and financial operations within Singapore, the company ensures external advertising and B2B portal yields are not double-taxed in India under Place of Effective Management (POEM) rules.
8.3 Public Market Protections and Year 5 IPO Path
The business model builds toward a 20% Initial Public Offering (IPO) in Year 5, utilizing a Further Public Offering (FPO) strategy later on to expand to 2,500 cities. To protect corporate stability from public market short sellers, 60% of the Pre-IPO tranche is reserved exclusively for internal supply chain partners, domestic vendors, and registered ecosystem families.
8.4 Long-Term Civilizational Governance Shift
The final goal of the project shifts away from centralized corporate oversight. Once city construction, local food supply loops, and energy networks achieve stable operation, complete municipal and civilizational governance is transferred directly to the resident users of each smart city, creating a self-managed, distributed urban ecosystem.
